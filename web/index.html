How it works
  1. Rows are grouped into service lines (account × service × region × usage type) and summed by day. Only usage rows count when the file has a charge type column; purchases, taxes and credits are totalled separately.
  2. Each line gets an expected cost per day: the median of its last normal days, adjusted for weekdays versus weekends. Days already flagged are left out, so an ongoing problem does not become normal.
  3. A day is flagged when it sits above the robust z threshold and is at least the minimum extra per day above normal. A second test catches slow creep: a week averaging 25% above the month before while still rising. A line that appears from nothing is flagged as new spend.
  4. Each event is explained: extra cost, usage or price (when the file has quantities), its share of the account's increase, and the closest of ten known cost patterns with checks to run.

A line needs about five weeks of history before it can be scored. Lines whose highest day is below the minimum extra per day are skipped. The sample bill is synthetic, for a made-up retailer, with eight planted problems.
