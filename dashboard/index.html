How the explainer works, and the limits of this test
  1. Baseline. Every service line (account × service × region × usage type) gets its own expected cost for each day: the median of the last 28 normal days, adjusted for weekdays versus weekends. Days already flagged are left out, so an ongoing problem does not become "normal".
  2. Detection. A day is flagged when its cost sits more than 4 robust standard deviations above that baseline and is at least $40 above it. A second test catches slow creep: the last 7 days average 25% above the month before and keep rising.
  3. Explanation. Flagged days are joined into one event. The explainer measures the extra cost, splits it into a usage change and a price change, works out its share of the account's increase, and matches it against a playbook of nine known cost patterns (for example forgotten GPUs, NAT Gateway traffic, Savings Plan expiry). The matched pattern supplies the likely cause and the checks.
  4. Business calendar. Spikes during planned events, such as a sale, are kept but marked as expected instead of alerting.

Limits. The bill is synthetic and the anomalies were planted by the same author who wrote the detector and the playbook, so the scores show the method works as designed, not how it would do on a real bill. The explanations are rule-based templates, not a language model. On real data the playbook would need tuning, and planned events would need to be kept up to date.
